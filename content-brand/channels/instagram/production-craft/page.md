# Production Craft

Pam’s Instagram production craft turns a premise into a shoot-ready reel by making the format, opening, story shape, proof, sound, and physical setup deliberate before filming.^\[1\]^ (`data:27`)

## Operating model

The craft layer is a durable reference for why short-form moves work, rather than a workflow to walk through by hand. The default path from saved reels to specified ideas is board-ideation: saved references are decomposed into a measured parts-bin, grounded against first-party material, recombined into briefs, and graduated into the pipeline with provenance.^\[2\]^ (`data:27`) When there is no board to decompose, or when one video needs a deeper pass, shoot-prep is the manual fallback.^\[3\]^ (`data:27`)

Format selection is a separate planning axis from both the platform vessel and the reel’s internal retention structure. A reel can be the vessel, greenscreen the content style, and expert-debunk the internal structure; choose the style before designing the beats rather than treating a format label as a structure.^\[4\]^ (`data:77`)

An open-ended starting catalog contains roughly 28 named styles, including yapping, storytelling, educational, multitasking, greenscreen, reaction, voiceover, comparisons, transformation, before-and-afters, and step-by-step. Its five tradeoff axes—effort, frequency, speed of result, planning, and niche fit—help match a style to available production effort, desired speed, and whether the niche is visual or verbal. Example reach figures are uncontrolled signals, not comparative performance evidence; no format is universally best.^\[5\]^ (`data:77`)

A finished idea has six fields: format, main insight, hook, storyline, retention, and visual plan covering shooting, editing, and location.^\[6\]^ (`data:27`) The pattern banks explain what a move is and why it works; the measured library supplies reusable moves ranked by observed reach. Treat generic named templates as preparation prompts, not as proof that a formula will perform.^\[7\]^ (`data:27`)

## Format variations

Activity plus voiceover is a reusable variation: pair teaching with an authentic parallel activity, or narrate over relevant footage from the period being described, instead of relying on a static talking head. The visual layer can add authenticity, evidence, or rhythm, but the performance claim is explicitly untested; treat the pattern as a hypothesis rather than a finding.^\[8\]^ (`data:67`)

Keep this distinct from prop-based curiosity: the activity is a background-authenticity device, not an ambiguous tension mechanism. Cooking while teaching and dog fetch are working variants; fetch can supply a repeatable visual beat or pacing timer, while whether it should drive the edit and which activity fits the creator’s actual life remain open production decisions.^\[9\]^ (`data:67`)

## Hook design

A hook is an opening mechanism, not merely a line of copy. Design the first three seconds as three coordinated but non-duplicative surfaces: the visual layer establishes the literal proof object or action, the written layer makes the frame scannable, and the spoken line supplies tension or promise. Choose the mechanism before the wording—curiosity, challenge, proof, surprise, pain diagnosis, or compressed authority—and make the body clear the expectation the opening creates.^\[10\]^ (`data:206`)

Keep the hook to two sentences maximum, with the tension in the second sentence or the second half of the first.^\[11\]^ (`data:205`) Test structural moves against the spine instead of choosing a formula in advance: a counter-intuitive reframe, a specific-number contradiction, a direct promise with an immediate complication, an outcome paired with three pain points, or a visual-ambiguity cold open are useful candidates.^\[12\]^ (`data:205`)

Sound-off founder-pressure variants are appropriate only when the audience instantly recognizes the premise. Put the absurd burden in the caption and let one literal visual reversal carry the payoff: becoming a TikToker to market a startup can be paired with a small camera, while a co-founder taking two seconds for personal life can be met with borrowed Batman surveillance footage. Both hooks name the burden; the visual does not have to explain it.^\[13\]^ (`data:206`)

For everyday AI-team friction, the caption must identify a specific shared tension before a repeated acted beat or borrowed reaction can read as the payoff; the hook is the precise work situation, not the borrowed visual.^\[14\]^ (`data:206`) A precise work-friction POV caption can name that situation immediately—“POV: Your cofounder who can't code has a 'quick idea'”—so the following acted repetition or reaction is understood without setup. An escalating in-group callout instead stacks increasingly specific second-person questions, such as “Dude, you're not vibe coding?” followed by “You're not up till 3am coding with Claude Code?”; use it to identify a current in-group and invite momentum, not to replace proof or nuance.^\[15\]^ (`data:205`)

A familiar-question bait-and-switch works from the verbal side: borrow the curiosity of a recognizable social question, then repay it immediately with an unexpected, concrete technical subject—“Do you want to know why he's not texting you right now?” redirected straight into a bounded Claude Code MVP workflow. The familiar question supplies a broad curiosity gap, and the specific engineering reveal earns it by delivering a real process rather than prolonging the tease. Use the pattern only when the reveal leads directly into a useful mechanism with visible proof, the surface question and real subject share a credible tension, and the real topic arrives within the next beat; a disconnected curiosity hook becomes bait.^\[16\]^ (`data:205`) ^\[17\]^ (`data:206`)

## Story shape and retention

Plot the emotional arc before choosing a storyline or writing the script. Sketch emotion against time as a roller coaster—tension rises, releases briefly, rises again, and then earns the payoff—rather than a flat line or a single late spike.^\[18\]^ (`data:204`) Once the arc is clear, choose the container and capture its beats as touch-points before wording; the final script is a speaking base to adapt during the take, not text to memorize.^\[19\]^ (`data:204`)

Retention is structural: start near the action, deliver an early payoff, release briefly, and create another useful peak. Each beat should advance diagnosis, method, example, or application.^\[20\]^ (`data:207`) Suitable containers include framework lessons, case-study breakdowns, monologue insights, workflow demos, episodic continuations, and compressed advice stacks; natural delivery may hide the progression, while complex teaching benefits from explicit labels.^\[21\]^ (`data:207`)

Retention devices come after the hook rather than replacing it. Use a verbal chapter marker, a two-failure-scenario game, a peak-release-peak arc, or an early promise whose method is delivered later when the structure calls for a mid-reel reset or a reason to keep watching.^\[22\]^ (`data:205`)

For ultra-short POV humor, put the recognizable belief or state in the caption, use one restrained repeated or contradictory action, and end as soon as the joke is understood. Founder-pressure versions use the same grammar: the caption contrasts company-building with the unwanted TikTok-marketing job or specifies the tiny personal-life act, while the camera or borrowed surveillance footage makes the reaction disproportionate. Do not add a lesson or CTA that changes the format.^\[23\]^ (`data:207`)

Two post-hook variants extend that grammar. Captioned friction can resolve through a continuous escalation or a hard cut to an exaggerated reaction, ending as soon as the payoff lands. An in-group callout can escalate identity questions, puncture them with a self-aware downside, and restore momentum with a concise shared conclusion; use this short monologue only when the shared behavior itself is entertaining, and make the self-aware turn arrive before the rallying tone becomes empty hype.^\[24\]^ (`data:204`) ^\[25\]^ (`data:207`)

A bounded build loop is the build-in-public storyline counterpart: show one phase implemented, deployed, sanity-checked, and then explicitly authorized before the next begins.^\[26\]^ (`data:207`) What makes an AI-assisted build credible is the repeated, human-gated loop rather than an implied uninterrupted autonomous build—deployment and testing alone are not proof, since the next phase starts only after the operator approves the prior one.^\[27\]^ (`data:204`) ^\[28\]^ (`data:207`) Use it for build-in-public content with real phase artifacts, a deployable increment, and an honest verification checkpoint, and avoid it when there is no concrete evidence for each step.^\[29\]^ (`data:204`)

## Visual proof and production design

Visuals are a preparation decision at the same level as the script. The governing question is: what does the viewer need to see to believe the claim? If the story itself is the proof, decide that explicitly rather than leaving the visual layer for the edit.^\[30\]^ (`data:28`)

Choose the proof object before polishing the script: a prop, UI capture, diagram, before-and-after sequence, screenshot, or other artifact. Match the narrowest evidence to the claim and, where relevant, show input, mechanism, and result; product education should cut from each spoken consequence to the interface state or artifact that demonstrates that exact consequence, not to a generic dashboard montage.^\[31\]^ (`data:208`)

The five visual types have distinct jobs: pure talking head for personal, emotional, or insight-led pieces; physical prop for a visual hook and an ambiguous stake; drawn diagram for a structural concept; screen capture for product, workflow, or result claims; and overlay text as a supporting label or clarification, never as the proof itself.^\[32\]^ (`data:28`) A prop can also provide a stable in-frame anchor for a continuous take, while a diagram or screen capture should be planned in its reveal or capture order.^\[33\]^ (`data:28`)

Meme visuals have a different proof job from product demos. The text establishes the recognizable interpretation, while the visual supplies only the contradiction or escalation. For founder-pressure gags, a small camera literalizes the unwanted second job and borrowed Batman footage functions as an overreaction; it is a punchline, not proof that founders should work without personal lives. A continuous acted scene can carry the punchline when the caption has already named the cofounder-work tension, while a borrowed reaction should follow only after the text has made its emotional target unambiguous.^\[34\]^ (`data:208`)

Product-switch gags follow the same rule: a persistent caption naming the switch—ChatGPT to Claude Max in the source example—makes an exaggerated borrowed reaction legible, with the caption carrying the product context and the reaction supplying the shared emotional payoff. The product judgment is not argued; in-group recognition is the complete joke. Give the reaction one precise captioned condition and no extra product claims—the visual is an in-group feeling marker, not evidence that the switch improves outcomes.^\[35\]^ (`data:208`) ^\[36\]^ (`data:207`) ^\[37\]^ (`data:208`)

The overlay preference is flat and editorial: opaque surfaces, thin borders, strong typography, and negative space. Avoid glow, drop shadow, blur, translucency, glassmorphism, and simulated floating depth; use the cards to combine only the claim and evidence needed for the current beat, alternating them with clean talking-head stretches.^\[38\]^ (`data:28`)

Before the camera rolls, lock four things: the visual type; exactly what appears and when; the physical setup required; and whether the visual removes the need for cuts. If any one is undecided, the prep card is incomplete.^\[39\]^ (`data:28`) In the edit, keep a stable presenter or scaffold and change the evidence surface at the semantic beat rather than adding motion for its own sake. A practical planning range is a new evidence state about every five to eight seconds, with longer holds when one diagram supports several sentences; check that the first frame identifies the category and that the sequence still makes sense with sound low or muted.^\[40\]^ (`data:208`)

## Audio choices

Organize background music by production job, content type, or emotional register, then audition tracks inside the relevant bucket. Music can support pacing, tone, familiarity, or contrast, but performance language about a song is not causal evidence of reach.^\[41\]^ (`data:80`) Keep discovery metadata, reference audio, and publication rights separate; before publishing, verify the license and the platform and account restrictions for the actual post.^\[42\]^ (`data:80`)

## Shoot-ready gate

A reel is ready to film when the six-field brief is complete, the hook mechanism and three opening-layer jobs are explicit, the emotional arc and container have been reduced to beat touch-points, each claim has a matching proof object and timing, the physical setup and cut plan are locked, and the audio choice has passed the applicable rights check.^\[43\]^ (`data:27`) ^\[44\]^ (`data:206`) ^\[45\]^ (`data:28`) ^\[46\]^ (`data:80`) This order keeps craft decisions upstream of filming while leaving delivery flexible enough to sound like a person rather than a recited template.^\[47\]^ (`data:27`)
