# Founder Content Strategy

Founder Content Strategy is Amir’s personal lane for communicating the work of building AI systems. It covers personal brand, thought leadership, and build-in-public documentation, intentionally rawer and more in-the-moment than Pam’s company account.^\[1\]^ (`data:25`) The company account handles trend-riding and brand voice; this lane handles the founding story, teaching, and personal reality.^\[2\]^ (`data:25`)

## Position and audience

Amir’s creator position is AI practitioner / founder building in public, specifically around problems that emerge when someone is actually building and operating AI systems rather than theorising about them.^\[3\]^ (`data:92`) It speaks to two audiences: ambitious, often young people without traditional CS backgrounds who want to build with AI, and business owners, freelancers, managers, and employees who want AI productivity but feel locked out.^\[4\]^ (`data:25`) The account is explicitly not positioned for engineers.^\[5\]^ (`data:25`)

The differentiated tension is the management burden, not the novelty of the tools: “I was the AI’s manager, and it was exhausting.”^\[6\]^ (`data:92`) Pam’s underlying why gives that tension a founder-specific shape: entrepreneurship requires knowing which action has the most leverage while being both the worker and the manager.^\[7\]^ (`data:21`) Amir’s own journey supplies the first-party version of the problem: he built Pam for his own failure mode; his strength is going deep fast into learning, building, and problem-solving, while the cost is losing sight of the whole picture.^\[8\]^ (`data:35`)

Positioning starts with a specific viewer frustration, assumption, or desired change, then frames the piece as a diagnosis or mechanism rather than a broad category or biography.^\[9\]^ (`data:104`) Communication should lead with the world, feeling, and wound before naming the solution.^\[10\]^ (`data:21`) Pam can be named, but the current rule is to lead with the problem and lived experience; build-in-public content is about the work and lesson, not a demo or sales pitch.^\[11\]^ (`data:25`)

## Editorial portfolio

The recurring portfolio has four pillars:

- **Building in public** — what Amir actually builds: Pam, the Company Brain, automations, and experiments, including the experience, lessons, failures, emotions, and motivations behind them.^\[12\]^ (`data:25`)
- **Education on AI engineering** — frameworks, insights, breakdowns, and practical explanations of why or how to do something, including comparisons and the clone format.^\[13\]^ (`data:25`)
- **Honest signal on AI** — direct opinions on use cases, the future, and what others are doing.^\[14\]^ (`data:25`)
- **Personal story** — trends, day-in-the-life material, and low-effort TikTok-style pieces.^\[15\]^ (`data:25`)

The relative weights of those pillars are not set.^\[16\]^ (`data:25`) The portfolio should combine education, personal or business storytelling, and authority or results-focused proof: education teaches, storytelling creates identification, and authority proof establishes trust.^\[17\]^ (`data:86`)

The strongest recurring topics are owned tensions: drift and lost context, the demo-to-production gap, custom tooling, and the build-in-public founder arc.^\[18\]^ (`data:92`) The custom-tooling thesis is simple: “Software should adapt to you, not you to the software.”^\[19\]^ (`data:105`) AI-agent governance can be used as an editorial angle, but without a documented Amir deployment incident it remains a market observation rather than an Amir-specific claim.^\[20\]^ (`data:97`) Likewise, an “AI hype versus reality” post needs direct standing, a reversed prediction, or a real internal data point; generic skepticism is not enough.^\[21\]^ (`data:143`)

## Packaging and voice

Packaging should make lived experience the differentiator rather than reproduce generic AI advice. A useful operator format is a dense stack of lessons from shipped products, agent failures, workflow mistakes, or founder operations.^\[22\]^ (`data:82`) For educational pieces, lead with the business outcome before explaining the infrastructure or mechanism.^\[23\]^ (`data:85`) Personal experience, unique perspectives, and contrarian takes matter more than information alone.^\[24\]^ (`data:91`) An easy/hard contrast is a reusable hook: name the widely adopted step, then surface the overlooked next step.^\[25\]^ (`data:84`) Founder pressure is also a fit for a sub-15-second POV: put the recognizable state in the caption, show one restrained repeated or contradictory action, and end as soon as the joke is understood; do not add a lesson or CTA that changes the format.^\[26\]^ (`data:207`)

Amir’s voice is direct, founder-led, technically credible, and free of corporate speak.^\[27\]^ (`data:38`) He supplies the substance and voice; support consists of brainstorming, research, and restrained shaping, not writing from scratch, restructuring, or replacing his voice with cleaner prose.^\[28\]^ (`data:15`)

## First-party source material

The journey directory is the raw source layer for this strategy, not a collection of finished posts. It captures moments, lessons, failures, decisions, and stories as they happen, then feeds Instagram build-in-public content, LinkedIn founder posts, and future X threads.^\[29\]^ (`data:29`) Its `founding-story.md` file is the retroactive origin story and raw material for the first build-in-public arc, while `log.md` is the ongoing capture log.^\[30\]^ (`data:29`)

The log records dated, tagged raw material and is designed to be shaped into content later; its types are moment, lesson, failure, decision, and milestone.^\[31\]^ (`data:35`) The founding-story capture defines a four-to-six-post arc that takes the audience from Pam’s beginning to its current story.^\[32\]^ (`data:37`) This preserves a repeatable source-to-story pipeline without asking the founder to manufacture polished content in advance.^\[33\]^ (`data:35`)

## Channels and formats

Channel priority has an explicit conflict in the inputs. Amir’s member profile says “Primary channel: LinkedIn,” while the operating plan was rewritten on 2026-08-25 from the 2026-08-21 and 2026-08-25 strategy sessions and now lists Instagram as primary and LinkedIn as second.^\[34\]^ (`data:15`)^\[35\]^ (`data:46`) This page follows the later dated operating plan as the current priority, while keeping LinkedIn as a separate secondary founder lane.

- **Instagram is primary for audience building.** The current vessel is reels only; the plan schedules five reels a week on Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Friday, and Sunday.^\[36\]^ (`data:46`)^\[37\]^ (`data:25`) Its backbone is talking-head material and build-in-public storytelling, with rotating reach experiments and a very-low-effort tier for a projected miss.^\[38\]^ (`data:46`)
- **LinkedIn is secondary.** Amir’s lane uses a direct founder voice, with a two-post weekly floor and four-post target; its post days and execution cycle remain parked pending platform research.^\[39\]^ (`data:38`) Current LinkedIn observations favour native 200–500-word posts, a Problem–Insight–Implication structure, and specificity over abstraction.^\[40\]^ (`data:138`)
- **X and YouTube Shorts are not current commitments.** X is explicitly parked as of 2026-08-25 because its daily-posting plan is not credible on top of the Instagram and LinkedIn commitments.^\[41\]^ (`data:47`) The retained X thinking is for reconsideration, not current volume.^\[42\]^ (`data:47`) YouTube Shorts are also parked in the current operating plan.^\[43\]^ (`data:46`)

The format decision is deliberately made before the topic.^\[44\]^ (`data:46`) The current Instagram architecture separates the vessel from the audience-recognizable format and internal retention structure, while keeping reels as the opening vessel.^\[45\]^ (`data:46`) Channel-specific execution belongs on [Instagram](../channels/instagram/page.md), [LinkedIn](../channels/linkedin/page.md), and [Twitter](../channels/twitter/page.md); cross-channel operating choices belong on [Content Strategy](../content-strategy/page.md), and reusable briefs and production mechanics belong on [Content Development](../content-development/page.md).

## Validation and steering

The niche remains a hypothesis rather than a proven audience position: there is no direct post-performance data because no posts have yet been published and measured.^\[46\]^ (`data:92`) The current plan separates controllable output from outcomes: five Instagram reels a week, two to four LinkedIn posts a week, and one lead magnet a week, with 90-day targets of 10,000 average reel views and 5,000 LinkedIn followers.^\[47\]^ (`data:46`)

Validation should optimise for audience quality, not a follower total alone. Useful signals are relevant replies, recognized expertise, qualified conversations, or a durable association with the point of view.^\[48\]^ (`data:104`) External scouting supplies hypotheses for experiments: one LinkedIn run found counter-intuitive reframes and first-person narratives paired with concrete outcomes recurring across high-performing posts.^\[49\]^ (`data:139`) Another found production maturity converging on the question of how to make a demo work when real users arrive.^\[50\]^ (`data:140`) The current platform synthesis turns those observations into the native-post defaults of specificity, strong hooks, and the Problem–Insight–Implication structure, while noting that generic AI-future takes did not reach the top tier.^\[51\]^ (`data:138`)

Still unresolved are the pillar weights, LinkedIn’s execution cycle, which candidate sub-niche actually resonates, and what Amir-specific evidence can support the governance angle.^\[52\]^ (`data:25`)^\[53\]^ (`data:38`)^\[54\]^ (`data:92`)^\[55\]^ (`data:97`)
