# Content Strategy

PamAI's communications start from the human problem rather than a feature list.^\[1\]^ (`data:21`) Publishing is channel-first: each platform has its own accounts, playbook, and working space, while shared doctrine and cross-channel material stay at the root.^\[2\]^ (`data:22`)

## Strategic premise

PamAI's core narrative is about direction: the hard part of building is knowing which action brings the most leverage, not a lack of effort or intelligence.^\[3\]^ (`data:21`) Every message should lead with the audience's changing world, existing feeling, and wound, then name what addresses it.^\[4\]^ (`data:21`)

Market-positioning content makes fragmentation across tools, drift, and the need to re-explain context legible rather than turning the feed into a competitor comparison.^\[5\]^ (`data:20`) Public posts should describe the pattern, not name competitors or products directly.^\[6\]^ (`data:20`) Amir's build-in-public content may name Pam, but it still leads with the problem and lived experience, not a demo or sales pitch.^\[7\]^ (`data:25`)

## Account roles and recurring pillars

The portfolio gives each account a distinct job instead of treating every channel as one repurposed feed.

- **PAM on LinkedIn** uses The Problem to make founder drift, fog, and split legible without product mention.^\[8\]^ (`data:44`) The POV takes a position on tools, work, AI, and management.^\[9\]^ (`data:44`) The Proof shows concrete product moments, the daily cycle, and real use cases to create waitlist interest.^\[10\]^ (`data:44`)
- **Amir's personal lane** is rawer, personal, and build-in-public oriented. Its defined audiences are ambitious people without a traditional CS background who want to build with AI, and business owners, freelancers, managers, and employees who want AI productivity; it does not target engineers.^\[11\]^ (`data:25`) Its four pillars are building in public, education on AI engineering, honest signal on AI, and personal story.^\[12\]^ (`data:51`) The guardrail is receipts: state the specific thing, what it took, and what did not work, rather than making general AI promises.^\[13\]^ (`data:25`)
- **Pam on Instagram** is the brand-reach channel, participating in platform culture through trends, humour, visual storytelling, and relatable moments rather than acting as a founder account.^\[14\]^ (`data:26`) Its pillars are trend-riding, founder reality from Pam's voice, and natural product teases.^\[15\]^ (`data:26`) Its voice is dry, funny, and self-aware: relatable first, brand second, and distinct from Amir's personal account.^\[16\]^ (`data:26`)
- **Negin on LinkedIn** is a separate professional lane about product design judgment, defensible product positions, and decision rationale; Pam is the evidence rather than the plot.^\[17\]^ (`data:40`) Her delivery is essayistic and argument-led, not a hook/listicle formula.^\[18\]^ (`data:40`)
- **Teaching content** explains high-level, business-side AI concepts to normal business people, translating technical ideas into plain language rather than teaching Pam's internals.^\[19\]^ (`data:42`)

Recurring themes can graduate from research when repetition supports a durable editorial lane. AI Hype vs. Reality is treated as a distinct theme, and it requires credibility and evidence rather than generic skepticism.^\[20\]^ (`data:143`)

## Channel architecture and cadence

The doctrine has two clocks. Niche material answers who and what; strategy answers how much, when, and in what form; channel files adapt both for one platform, while playbooks hold craft.^\[21\]^ (`data:22`)

The current platform priority is Instagram first, LinkedIn second, with Twitter/X and YouTube Shorts parked in the current operating plan.^\[22\]^ (`data:46`) For Amir's personal channels, the plan sets an Instagram floor of 3 reels a week and a target of 5, a LinkedIn floor of 2 posts and a target of 4, and one lead magnet each week.^\[23\]^ (`data:46`) Instagram's five reel days are Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Friday, and Sunday.^\[24\]^ (`data:25`) Monday is the full edit, ideation, and lead-magnet day; Thursday and Saturday are shoot days, and the one-week rolling lag keeps finished inventory ahead of publication.^\[25\]^ (`data:46`)

The PAM company LinkedIn page runs 1–3 posts per week, rotating through The Problem, The POV, and The Proof; personal accounts sit on top of that company cadence.^\[26\]^ (`data:44`) The PAM Instagram playbook sets 2–3 weekday pieces and coordinates its batch production with Amir's personal account on the same shoot days, using different content.^\[27\]^ (`data:26`)

LinkedIn remains a deliberate exception: its execution cycle is parked as of 2026-08-25 pending platform research, even though its volume, separate-from-Instagram approach, shared weekly lead magnet, and independent streak are decided.^\[28\]^ (`data:45`) Post days, writing time, source material, and the mechanics that actually work on the platform remain open.^\[29\]^ (`data:45`)

## Workflow and governance

The publishing pipeline treats a post as one state-and-content row, keeps `posts.content` and `posts.content_brief` current, and binds Instagram production files back to the row; ideas come from sessions, the journey material well, or promoted wiki research.^\[30\]^ (`data:22`)

Niyayesh owns content creation and execution across the platforms.^\[31\]^ (`data:19`) Once a piece has its main insight, she synthesizes the weekly list from research, journey material, and brainstorming into 10 ideas and 5 chosen pieces, then owns format selection, visual planning, scheduling, and execution follow-through.^\[32\]^ (`data:19`) A shoot-ready idea has six required fields: format, main insight, hook, storyline, retention, and visual plan.^\[33\]^ (`data:25`)

Every PAM and personal LinkedIn post receives one review before publishing—not as a gate, but as a second pair of eyes for voice consistency.^\[34\]^ (`data:44`) Each piece also gets a job—discovery, authority, relationship, or conversion—so its opening, proof, next step, and review metric are chosen together.^\[35\]^ (`data:99`) The operating loop is to define the audience problem and position, choose a reusable format and job, build the opening and proof, publish into a route, and review the result against its job.^\[36\]^ (`data:94`) Review separates attention, trust, conversion, and format failures, changing one major variable at a time rather than turning a single result into a permanent rule.^\[37\]^ (`data:94`)

AI can compress research, production, repurposing, and reporting only after the strategic choices are made.^\[38\]^ (`data:94`) In consequential workflows, AI handles aggregation and pattern detection while humans retain approval.^\[39\]^ (`data:102`)

Platform research is directional, not timeless doctrine. A June 17 LinkedIn scout run observed stronger performance for founder voice than advisor or consultant voice and near-zero engagement for short teaser-with-link posts; these observations should inform tests without replacing the pending research round.^\[40\]^ (`data:141`)

## Editorial voice and quality bar

PAM's LinkedIn voice is short, one idea per line, specific, concrete, calm, and direct; it avoids startup-ese, productivity rhetoric, emoji as punctuation, and selling.^\[41\]^ (`data:44`) Amir's audience doctrine similarly prioritises specific honest takes, real examples, what does not work, and operational intelligence over generic advice.^\[42\]^ (`data:51`) The teaching bank is raw input rather than a ranked list of beliefs: recent captures carry recency bias and should be synthesized over time.^\[43\]^ (`data:42`)

The shortest durable rule is why before what, evidence before hype, and a distinct voice for each account. The account-specific operating detail lives in [Instagram](../channels/instagram/page.md), [LinkedIn](../channels/linkedin/page.md), and [Founder Content Strategy](../founder-content-strategy/page.md); production craft lives in [Content Development](../content-development/page.md), and AI workflow principles live in [AI Marketing Workflows](../ai-marketing-workflows/page.md).

## Open dependency

The missing infrastructure for LinkedIn is not another report; it is a browsable, persistent, tagged Content Library that can be searched and synthesized against.^\[44\]^ (`data:45`)
