# Business Model

The Product Domain covers product philosophy, feature rationale, and scale/growth thinking under Negin’s ownership as CEO and product lead.^\[1\]^ (`data:178`) PamAI’s commercial model remains a working product-business hypothesis rather than settled policy: its source document calls itself “an important, live document, not settled doctrine” and says it is under active review by Negin.^\[2\]^ (`data:63`)

## Create value

PamAI names its core value “cognitive coherence”: removing the gap between knowing what matters and actually working on it continuously, with relief from holding the entire project in mind every day.^\[3\]^ (`data:63`) The value is intended to compound into “compounding intelligence”—the longer Pam is used, the more it knows how a person thinks, decides, and drifts.^\[4\]^ (`data:63`) “Team coherence” is described as a future expansion of value and is not built.^\[5\]^ (`data:63`) The commercial defensibility thesis is methodology plus compounding memory rather than the underlying model.^\[6\]^ (`data:63`)

## Deliver value

The proposed delivery journey moves from project definition into an ongoing daily rhythm:

1. **Define** — a conversational Foundation session extracts seven fields and builds two outputs: a plan for the user and a mind for Pam.^\[7\]^ (`data:63`)
2. **Confirm** — the user reads the plan, pushes back if it does not resonate, and Pam edits it.^\[8\]^ (`data:63`)
3. **Reality setup** — Pam connects the calendar and allocates the project into the user’s actual week.^\[9\]^ (`data:63`)
4. **Daily rhythm** — a morning brief selects three tasks from the plan and calendar, chosen by Pam rather than the user.^\[10\]^ (`data:63`)

The delivery extensions include a downloadable and shareable documentation home described as “Pam’s memory, and also yours. No scattered Google Docs.”^\[11\]^ (`data:63`) Collaboration currently means two individual Pam users sharing one project, explicitly not team management; the stated purpose is to prepare users for Team Pam before that feature exists.^\[12\]^ (`data:63`) The proposed delivery moat is that Pam becomes the only place the project lives—thinking, tracking, documents, and sharing in one continuous environment—rather than merely a chat interface or morning brief.^\[13\]^ (`data:63`) The proposed channel is founder-to-founder and community-seeded: the value is “hard to advertise, easy to feel once described” and spreads through testimony.^\[14\]^ (`data:63`)

## Capture value

Bring your own subscription is the immediate economic wedge, not the full product thesis.^\[15\]^ (`data:62`) The model says using a customer’s eligible Claude or future agent subscription makes deeper context engineering and agentic work economically viable.^\[16\]^ (`data:62`)

Capture is intended to follow what Pam actually does rather than seats, but the implementation remains open because a visible usage meter may conflict with the promise to show up daily and trust the rhythm.^\[17\]^ (`data:63`) Pricing is not settled: the housekeeping index calls credit pricing “stale” and “removed entirely,”^\[18\]^ (`data:70`) while a 2026-08-15 entry says the mechanism is credit-based again^\[19\]^ (`data:63`) and states a price of £36/month for 1,000 credits.^\[20\]^ (`data:63`) That entry says the 1,000 credits are designed to cover roughly 25 days to a month of regular use^\[21\]^ (`data:63`) and explicitly frames the price as initial-testing pricing rather than the finalized structure.^\[22\]^ (`data:63`) The working rationale is that £36 is not priced as an add-on cost.^\[23\]^ (`data:63`)

Team and multi-seat monetization remain unresolved; the model describes collaboration as “the on-ramp” while team capture is “the unanswered half.”^\[24\]^ (`data:63`)
