# Legal Structure

PamAI has no corporate entity yet: its privacy policy (version 1.0, effective 1 July 2026) names Amirmohammad Valizadehmehrabani as the operator, "an individual (not yet incorporated)", and as the controller of the personal data PamAI processes, with a business contact address at Bahariyeli Street No. 26, Apartment 2, Goztepe, Kadikoy, Istanbul, 34700, Turkey.^\[1\]^[^data:9] Incorporation timing, jurisdiction and entity shape are open decisions — a working exploration, not settled doctrine.^\[2\]^[^data:265]

## The open decision and the Innovator Founder visa constraint

The working exploration (a README filed as input for Amir, with the underlying ChatGPT conversation shared verbatim by Negin on 2026-08-28) started from a question from Negin: whether registering PAM as a UK company now would affect her Innovator Founder visa eligibility (targeting a 2027 endorsement application), and which structure best supports both the visa case and a future VC raise.^\[3\]^[^data:266] The scenario it works through has Negin and Amir creating PAM Ltd together in August 2026.^\[4\]^[^data:266]

Its stated conclusion: incorporating now does not hurt eligibility; what matters is keeping PAM the demonstrably core, scalable venture rather than letting it become a side product of a generic AI agency.^\[5\]^[^data:265] The README is explicit that no decision has been made yet on incorporation timing or company structure, that the analysis is ChatGPT's reading of Home Office guidance and not legal advice, and that it needs validation from an actual immigration solicitor and company formation advisor before anything here is treated as settled — real equity, IP and registration decisions sit downstream of it.^\[6\]^[^data:265]

On the rules themselves, the analysis reads the Home Office endorsement guidance as allowing "the sole founder or an instrumental member of the founding team, including when the business has already begun trading in the UK", so the simplified GOV.UK line that "you cannot join a business that is already trading" is aimed at joining someone else's pre-existing business, not at a founder working on the venture before endorsement — the endorsement template explicitly covers a founding member where the company has already begun trading.^\[7\]^[^data:266] The scenario it would avoid is the reverse: the guidance requires the innovation to be core to the business proposition, not bolted onto an otherwise ordinary business, hence the recommended framing PAM Ltd = PAM, with a trail from product development through customer discovery and pilots to paying users, intellectual property and a scalable SaaS business.^\[8\]^[^data:266]

## Candidate structures

Three architectures are worked through: a generic AI agency with PAM as a side product; a single company — PAM Technologies Ltd — designed as an AI product/technology company that also provides consulting services; and two separate entities, where a separate agency company is the potential home for a side consultancy run for cash flow.^\[9\]^[^data:266] The conversation ranks them for Innovator Founder strength, VC fit and complexity:^\[10\]^[^data:266]

| Structure | Innovator Founder | VC | Complexity |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI Agency Ltd + PAM as side product | weaker | weaker | Low |
| PAM Technologies Ltd + consulting arm | strong if structured well | strong if product becomes core | Low |
| Separate Agency Ltd + PAM Ltd | very clean | very clean | Higher |

For the founders' position today, the analysis calls the single-company option the sweet spot right now: one company, consulting revenue before PAM makes meaningful money, PAM's IP kept inside the company, and a clean product/VC/Innovator story.^\[11\]^[^data:266] The hierarchy that makes it work is PAM as the company's scalable core, with consulting as supporting revenue, customer discovery and implementation — documented properly, consulting engagements feed cash, customer insight and use cases into PAM pilots and product customers.^\[12\]^[^data:266]

The known cost is that inside one Ltd there is no legal wall between the agency and PAM: it is all one company, so consulting revenue, liabilities and PAM's IP all belong to the same entity, and investors buying shares effectively invest in the whole company, not merely the PAM product.^\[13\]^[^data:266] The analysis does not treat that as VC-ineligibility — PAM Technologies Ltd can absolutely raise VC money while also earning some consulting revenue, and the risk is only becoming potentially less attractive to some VCs if the company becomes too consultancy-heavy, since VC typically wants non-linear growth: revenue capable of growing much faster than headcount.^\[14\]^[^data:266] If a round demanded product-only exposure, the consulting operation might have to be separated, with contracts and IP transferred or the company restructured.^\[15\]^[^data:266]
